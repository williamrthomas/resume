## The shape of the bet I'm making

I'm betting that the strongest FDE candidates are *less* "engineers who can talk to customers" and *more* "operators who became engineers." The technical work for an FDE is real, but the differentiator is reading the customer org, picking the right scope, codifying patterns that don't fall apart when you leave the room, and shipping under ambiguity.

I've spent 9 years inside one enterprise (athena) doing exactly that — minus AI as the product. With Claude in the loop, AI is now what we ship, and the shape is the same.

## What I bring to engagements

- **Read the room.** I've worked with dental offices, legal firms, vocational schools, state agencies, ACOs, and athena platform partners. The shape of who's nervous, who's blocking, and who's hiding the actual constraint is familiar.
- **Codify patterns.** Pop Health support model and the enterprise data product operationalization both started as greenfield support disasters and became durable patterns. The same instinct is what makes deployment patterns transferable across customers.
- **Ship boring code.** systemd services, modular Python, structured-data APIs. I'd rather build something an FDE successor can pick up cleanly than something clever that needs me to interpret it.
- **Coach, don't grandstand.** 25+ years of coaching has taught me to find the smallest correction that moves someone, then let them keep going. Same muscle the FDE relationship needs.

## What I'd want from you in the interview

- A real customer scenario you've worked. Not a brain teaser — an actual ambiguous, multi-stakeholder, partially scoped engagement and how you handled it.
- What "shipping a deployment pattern" looks like at Anthropic in practice — what counts as a pattern, where they live, how reuse happens.
- The honest opinion on what makes someone burn out in an FDE role and what makes someone thrive.
